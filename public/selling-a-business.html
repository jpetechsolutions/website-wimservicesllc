Before you list

The preparation decides the outcome.

Most businesses that sit unsold for a year were not priced wrong so much as prepared poorly — or priced on a number nobody could substantiate.

The work that happens before a listing goes live is what a buyer's lender underwrites and what a buyer's accountant tests in diligence. Recast earnings that hold up. Records that reconcile. A lease with term left on it. A business that can run without you in the building every day. Each of those moves the price, and all of them are easier to fix before a buyer is watching.

  • Recast owner benefit and normalised earnings a lender will accept
  • Three years of financials reconciled to the tax returns
  • Customer concentration and key-person risk identified and addressed
  • Lease term, assignability and landlord consent confirmed early
  • Licences, permits and any transfer conditions mapped out

The process

Five stages, start to closing.

Typically six to twelve months. You will know which stage you are in and what is next at every point.

Consultation, financial review & valuation

A confidential conversation about your business, your goals and your timeline. We review roughly three years of financial statements plus current year-to-date results, recast owner benefit, and set a realistic market position — not a flattering number that stalls the listing for a year.

Positioning & confidential marketing

We prepare a confidential information memorandum and a buyer-ready listing that leads with the strengths a buyer underwrites. Marketing is discreet and targeted, so your employees, customers, landlord and competitors are not the ones who find out you are selling.

Buyer qualification

Every prospect is screened for seriousness and financial capacity. Before any detailed information is released, a buyer signs a non-disclosure agreement and provides proof of funds or demonstrated financing capability.

Offers, negotiation & due diligence

We evaluate offers on structure as well as headline price — cash at close, seller note, earn-out, holdbacks, working-capital treatment, lease assignment and transition terms. Then we support the buyer's diligence so it confirms what was represented.

Closing & transition

Closing is coordinated with experienced closing attorneys. Ownership transfers, funds are disbursed, and the handover — including training and any agreed consulting period — is managed so the business keeps running.

Confidentiality

The sale should be news only after it closes.

A leak costs real money. Key employees start looking, your best customers ask uncomfortable questions, suppliers tighten terms and competitors go recruiting. Every marketing decision is made with that risk in front of it.

  • Blind listings — no business name, no identifying photographs
  • Non-disclosure agreement executed before any detail is released
  • Proof of funds or demonstrated financing capability before financials
  • Buyer screening for seriousness, not just curiosity
  • Site visits scheduled so they do not disrupt the business

Seller questions

What owners ask before they commit.

What do you need from me to start?

Roughly three years of financial statements and tax returns, current year-to-date results, a copy of your lease, and an hour of conversation. That is enough to give you a realistic valuation range and tell you honestly whether now is the right time.

Will my employees or customers find out?

Not from us. Your business is marketed on a blind basis without its name, and identifying details are released only after a buyer signs an NDA and demonstrates financial capability. Protecting the business while it is for sale is part of the job, not an add-on.

What if my books are not clean?

Very common, and fixable — but better fixed before going to market than discovered in diligence. Recasting owner benefit, separating personal expenses and reconciling the accounting file are part of preparation. If the gap is large enough to affect value, the honest answer may be to spend a year tidying up first.

Do I have to stay on after closing?

Usually a short transition with training included in the sale. Many buyers want more, and that can be structured as a defined-term consulting agreement with the compensation set in the overall deal. What you do not want is an open-ended obligation agreed casually at closing.

Can the buyer get SBA financing?

Often, and it widens your buyer pool considerably. SBA lenders look at transferable cash flow, clean records, customer concentration, lease term and the collateral position. Businesses that can be pre-qualified by a lender before listing tend to attract more serious interest.
