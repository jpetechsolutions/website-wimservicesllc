Start here

The right business is a fit question before it is a price question.

A profitable business you are not suited to run is a bad acquisition at any multiple.

So the first conversation is about you: the capital you can deploy and still sleep, the industries you would genuinely run, whether you intend to operate day-to-day or hire a manager, where you want to be based, and what the deal has to return to justify leaving whatever you are doing now. Screening against that saves months of looking at the wrong companies.

The process

Six stages, search to closing.

Understanding your objectives

We start with what you actually want: investment range, industries you would and would not run, how hands-on you intend to be, where you want to live, and what return the deal has to produce for it to be worth doing.

Identifying the right opportunity

Sourcing runs through the Business Brokers of Florida co-brokerage network, the national listing platforms, private broker relationships and direct owner and advisor contacts — which is where off-market opportunities come from.

Confidential review & financial analysis

Historical financials, business and excise tax returns, year-to-date performance, accounting files, contracts, leases and inventory. A structured review built to verify that the numbers in the listing are the numbers in the business.

Financing & qualification

Guidance through SBA lending and seller financing, including which businesses are already lender pre-qualified. You will sign an NDA and show financing capability before detailed information is released — the same standard applied to every buyer.

Offers, negotiation & due diligence

We structure the offer, negotiate terms and manage the diligence period so issues surface while you can still price them, rather than after you own them.

Closing & transition

Closing with experienced attorneys, then the handover. Sellers typically provide a transition period with initial training included; extended support can be arranged as a defined-term consulting agreement.

Financing

How acquisitions actually get funded.

SBA acquisition loans

The most common route for Main Street deals. Lenders underwrite transferable cash flow, record quality, customer concentration, lease term and collateral. Some listings are pre-qualified by a lender before they reach the market.

Seller financing

A seller note can bridge a valuation gap, satisfy a lender's equity requirement and keep the previous owner invested in a clean transition. Terms, security and any offset rights matter as much as the rate.

Transition & training

Sellers typically include an initial training period in the sale. Extended help can be arranged as a defined-term consulting agreement, with compensation set inside the overall agreement rather than negotiated after closing.

Buyer questions

What buyers ask first.

How much cash do I need?

With SBA acquisition financing, buyers commonly put down a meaningful share of the purchase price, with the balance financed and sometimes a seller note filling part of the gap. You also need working capital after closing and reserves — the mistake first-time buyers make most often is spending every dollar on the down payment. Your lender will set the specifics; we will tell you early whether a deal is realistic.

What is a lender pre-qualified listing?

A business an SBA lender has already reviewed and indicated it would finance, subject to the buyer qualifying. It removes much of the uncertainty about whether the deal can be funded at all, which is the most common reason acquisitions die.

Can I see off-market businesses?

Sometimes. Sourcing runs through the Business Brokers of Florida co-brokerage network, the national listing platforms, private broker relationships and direct owner and advisor contacts. Owners who are quietly open to an offer but not formally listed come through the last of those.

What should I actually check in due diligence?

That the earnings are real and transferable. Financial statements reconciled to tax returns, bank deposits against reported revenue, customer concentration, the accounting file itself, contracts and their assignability, the lease, employee agreements, licences, inventory condition and any pending liabilities. We coordinate a structured review rather than a checklist skim.

Do I pay you as a buyer?

In most transactions the seller pays the brokerage fee, including on co-brokered deals. Where a buy-side engagement is fee-bearing, that is agreed in writing before any work begins — never discovered later.
