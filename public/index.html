How the practice works

Three principles, applied to every engagement.

Thoughtful preparation

Before anything goes to market, we build the case for your business — recast financials, clean numbers, defensible positioning and a clear story a buyer and a lender can both follow. Deals fall apart in due diligence when preparation was thin.

Clear communication

You get regular updates and the reasoning behind every recommendation, from listing through closing. No silence, no surprises, and a straight answer when the answer is not the one you were hoping for.

Disciplined execution

The goal is not the most offers — it is the right buyer at the right price on terms that actually survive closing and funding. That takes screening buyers properly and holding the line on structure.

Common questions

What owners and buyers ask first.

What is my business worth?

It depends on your recast earnings, how transferable the business is without you, the quality of your records, your industry's multiple range and current buyer and lender appetite. A business doing $500,000 of owner benefit with clean books and a management team is worth materially more than one doing the same earnings out of a shoebox.

The first conversation and the valuation discussion are confidential and come at no cost. You will get a realistic range and the reasoning behind it.

How long does it take to sell a business?

Most Main Street and lower middle-market sales run six to twelve months from listing to closing. Preparation quality, price realism, record keeping and whether the deal needs SBA financing are the biggest variables. Businesses that go to market well prepared close faster and at better terms than those that go out quickly and get repriced later.

How do you protect confidentiality?

Listings are marketed without identifying your business. Buyers receive general information first, and only sign-and-verify prospects — NDA executed plus proof of funds or financing capability — see financials, customer concentration, lease terms or your name. Employees, customers and competitors should learn about the sale from you, after it closes.

Do you work with buyers as well as sellers?

Yes. Buyer representation is a significant part of the practice, including first-time buyers using SBA financing and operators making an add-on acquisition. If a conflict would arise on a specific transaction, it is disclosed up front.

What does it cost?

Sell-side engagements work on a success fee at closing, agreed in writing before anything goes to market, with the term and any marketing costs stated plainly. There is no charge for the initial consultation or the valuation conversation.

What areas of Florida do you cover?

Florida statewide, from the Orlando office. Activity is deepest across Central Florida — Orange, Seminole, Osceola, Lake and Brevard — and along the I-4 corridor and the Gulf Coast through Hillsborough, Pinellas, Pasco, Sarasota and Manatee, with transactions also on the Treasure Coast and in the Panhandle.
